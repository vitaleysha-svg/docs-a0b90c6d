Software + service: what to steal, what to sell

Internal only · For Alex + Mladen · from two live demo calls (Foresight 09-15, Parker 09-16) · everything about them is what they said or showed on the call, not tested by us
Bottom line: both companies sell the same thing we want to sell: software with a strategist attached, priced on the customer's ad spend. Foresight does it with two founders by hand. Parker does it with AI and one support person, at $299 to $699 a month. Neither can see the whole market. We can. Put their service layer on top of our 13M ad library and we have an offer that earns a much higher price than $29 to $129.

The offer, first draft

1. Your creative strategist

From Foresight: the "do this" list + review calls
  • Onboarding call built around your brand
  • Every two weeks: what to make next, with the clones ready
  • Monthly review call on the top tier

2. The prompt library

From Parker: strategist-written training docs
  • The exact prompts our strategist uses: research, hooks, scripts, offers
  • Work in the Assistant and in Claude through our MCP
  • New ones added every month

3. The exclusive AI

From Parker: brand brain + scheduled reports
  • Knows your brand, your reviews, your spied brands
  • Sends your winners every week without you logging in
  • Runs on the whole market, which neither of them has

4. A human who answers

From both: shared Slack channel
  • Private Slack channel with our team
  • AI answers instantly around the clock, a person picks up inside a set response time
  • Parker runs this with one person. It can start thin

Why it holds the price: Parker gets $299 to $699 for a product 8 months old with no ad creation and no market view. Foresight sells two founders' time on a custom quote (their number is still pending). Both price on ad spend, so nobody compares it to a $29 tool. And every piece above builds up over time, so canceling means losing something. Today 45% of our new payers never make a second payment because they lose nothing by leaving.
